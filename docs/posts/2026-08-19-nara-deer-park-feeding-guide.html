中文

我曾親自走進奈良公園，也真的買過鹿仙貝餵鹿。照片裡的奈良小鹿總是一副溫馴、懂得鞠躬的模樣；但真正站在牠們面前，最先感受到的不是「可愛」，而是距離突然消失了。鹿一發現人手上有仙貝，原本散步、曬太陽的幾隻會立刻靠過來。那一刻我才明白：奈良的鹿雖然習慣人群，仍然是會搶食、會受驚，也會用頭或嘴巴表達不耐煩的野生動物。

這也是奈良公園最好玩的地方。餵鹿不是隔著圍欄觀看，而是直接進入一座人、鹿、寺院與森林共同生活的城市風景。只要先理解購買方法與互動規則，一束小小的鹿仙貝，就能成為認識奈良最好的開場。

先搞懂：奈良的鹿不是動物園裡的鹿

奈良的鹿長久以來被視為春日大社神明的使者「神鹿」，目前也是國家指定的天然紀念物。牠們與一般日本鹿是同一物種，主要食物是公園裡的青草、樹葉和橡實；鹿仙貝只是點心，不是正餐。這個觀念非常重要，因為看到鹿主動靠近，不代表可以擁抱、追逐、摸角或拿食物逗弄。

我的實際感受是：沒有拿食物時，鹿常常只是安靜地走過身邊；一旦拿出仙貝，互動節奏便會完全改變。與其急著拍照，先觀察附近有幾隻鹿、自己是否有退路，以及身旁有沒有幼童，會安全得多。秋季的雄鹿、帶著幼鹿的母鹿尤其需要保持距離；看到幼鹿也不要伸手摸。

鹿仙貝怎麼買？價格、地點與付款方式

截至 2026 年 8 月，官方公布的鹿仙貝售價是一束 10 片、200 日圓。原料主要是小麥粉與米糠，不加糖；部分收入會用於鹿的保護。公園主要步道、東大寺參道、浮雲園地周邊的露天攤位及部分紀念品店都能買到，攤位通常掛著「鹿せんべい」字樣，看到整疊圓形薄餅就是了。

現場購買通常最簡單：準備 100 日圓硬幣或小額紙鈔，向攤販說「しかせんべい、ひとつください」即可。各攤位營業時間會受天候、人潮與季節影響，傍晚不一定還有販售。如果較晚抵達，春日大社境內的鹿苑與春日大社停車場休息處設有鹿仙貝自動販賣機，一盒也是一束 10 片。自動販賣機狀態仍可能調整，別把它當成唯一購買方案。

我建議先走到較空曠的位置再拆，不要站在攤位正前方慢慢整理錢包。鹿知道哪裡在賣仙貝，一看到包裝就可能圍過來；手機、地圖、紙袋和塑膠袋要先收好，仙貝只拿在慣用手，另一手保留平衡。

我的餵鹿實戰：五個動作，把慌亂變成好玩的互動

  1. 先選一隻，不要一次面對整群。找外圍、動作較平靜的鹿，保持一個手臂以上的距離；如果旁邊已有四、五隻盯著仙貝，先移動到較空曠處。
  2. 拆掉紙製證紙，把剩餘仙貝藏好。不要拿著整疊在鹿面前數，也不要把包裝或地圖垂在身邊。鹿可能誤認為食物。
  3. 一片一片，伸平手掌，乾脆地餵。鹿已經看到仙貝時，不要故意抬高手或反覆收回。官方也提醒，遲遲不給可能引發咬、頂或拉扯。
  4. 不要追求每次都鞠躬。有些鹿會點頭或鞠躬，有些不會；互動重點是安全和尊重，不是逼牠完成表演。拍照的人應站在側面，不要讓餵食者為了鏡頭背對鹿群。
  5. 餵完立刻張開雙手。雙手手掌朝向鹿，清楚表示「沒有了」，再平穩離開。不要尖叫、奔跑或把空袋子甩來甩去。

親自餵過後，我最推薦的不是「一次買很多」，而是先買一束練習。十片看起來不多，實際上足以完成一輪互動。第一束餵完，知道鹿靠近的速度與自己的反應後，再決定是否加買，體驗通常會更從容。

奈良小鹿最迷人的，不是牠會不會鞠躬，而是古都真的為牠保留了一條共同生活的路。

最重要的禁忌：不要把善意變成鹿的負擔

  • 只餵鹿仙貝：麵包、餅乾、蔬菜、水果與自己的零食都不要餵。
  • 垃圾全部帶走：奈良公園沒有垃圾桶，塑膠袋、紙張和濕紙巾都要收進有拉鍊的包內。
  • 不要觸摸幼鹿：母鹿可能為了保護幼鹿突然攻擊；拍照也要保留距離。
  • 不要抓角、騎乘或從背後驚嚇：鹿的反應速度比人快，讓牠保有離開的空間。
  • 餵完洗手：避免邊餵邊吃自己的食物，回到餐廳前先使用肥皂清潔雙手。

如果被多隻鹿包圍，先把仙貝快速餵完或收妥，張開雙手，面向鹿緩慢移動；不要轉身奔跑。帶小孩時由大人持有仙貝，孩子站在大人前方或側邊共同餵食，避免孩子單獨拿著整束。

餵完鹿別急著走：周邊五個值得深入看的景點

1. 東大寺：從南大門走到大佛殿

東大寺不是只有大佛。從南大門仰望巨大的木構與金剛力士像，再走進大佛殿，空間尺度會讓人自然放慢腳步。2026 年官方資訊顯示，大佛殿 4–10 月開放 7:30–17:30、11–3 月 8:00–17:00；個人入堂成人 800 日圓，且現場只收現金。清晨先逛東大寺再餵鹿，可以避開中午最密集的人潮。

2. 二月堂：免費但常被忽略的奈良視角

從大佛殿東側往山坡走，二月堂的舞台能望向奈良市區。這段上坡不長，石階與古老土牆很有味道。若時間有限，我會把它排在不少付費景點之前：視野開闊、氣氛安靜，而且從東大寺順路可達。

3. 若草山：把奈良公園攤開在腳下

若草山山麓距東大寺不遠。2026 年資料為每年 3 月第 3 個星期六至 12 月第 2 個星期日、9:00–17:00 開山；成人 150 日圓、3 歲以上兒童 80 日圓。只走到一重目便已有視野，但坡度比遠看更陡，雨後與下坡時尤其要注意鞋底抓地力。

4. 春日大社與飛火野：鹿文化的核心

春日大社的朱紅社殿、森林與石燈籠，把「神鹿」的文化背景說得最完整。一般參拜所免費，御本殿特別參拜 9:00–16:00、700 日圓，但祭典期間可能限制。附近的飛火野是大片草地，早晨能看到較自然、分散的鹿群；特定季節還有「鹿寄せ」，出發前要查當年度日期。

5. 興福寺、猿澤池與奈良町：用老街收尾

回近鐵奈良站方向時，可依序經過興福寺與猿澤池，再走進奈良町。寺院與鹿群之後，町家、咖啡店、柿葉壽司和奈良漬讓旅程回到人的生活。如果同行者不想爬若草山，這條平緩路線也很適合替代。

我會這樣安排：半日版與一日深度版

半日經典版（約 4 小時）：近鐵奈良站 → 興福寺 → 奈良公園餵鹿 → 東大寺南大門與大佛殿 → 二月堂 → 原路回站。這條路把鹿、寺院與城市景觀一次看完，適合從京都或大阪當日往返。

一日深度版（約 7 小時）：近鐵奈良站 → 興福寺 → 浮雲園地餵鹿 → 東大寺 → 二月堂 → 若草山山麓／一重目 → 春日大社 → 飛火野 → 奈良町晚餐。完整走完約 8–10 公里，夏季要補水，冬季則要留意天黑時間。

最後的實用提醒

奈良公園本身 365 天、24 小時免費開放；近鐵奈良站步行約 8 分鐘，JR 奈良站步行約 20 分鐘或搭公車約 7–10 分鐘。公園面積超過 500 公頃，不要把它想成一個有明確入口的小公園。穿好走的鞋、帶現金、拉鍊包、飲水與簡單雨具，比安排太多打卡點更重要。

回想那次親自餵鹿，我最難忘的不是仙貝在幾秒內被吃完，而是餵食結束、雙手攤開後，鹿轉身走回草地，遠處仍是東大寺的屋頂和春日山的森林。奈良讓人看到的不是一場動物表演，而是一千多年來，人們如何在城市裡替野生動物留下一個位置。

ENGLISH

I have visited Nara Park in person and fed the deer with shika senbei. Photographs make the deer look endlessly gentle, almost as if each one is waiting to bow politely for a cracker. Standing among them feels different: the moment a deer notices the crackers in your hand, the comfortable distance between wildlife and visitor disappears. A few deer that had been grazing or resting may approach at once. They are accustomed to people, but they remain wild animals that compete for food, startle easily, and can use their mouths or antlers when impatient.

That directness is also what makes Nara special. There are no fences separating the deer from the temples, lawns, forests and city streets. Learn the rules first, and a small bundle of crackers becomes a memorable introduction to the entire landscape.

First, Remember That These Are Wild Deer

Nara's deer have long been protected as sacred messengers of Kasuga Taisha and are designated a national natural monument. Their normal diet is grass, leaves and acorns; deer crackers are treats, not meals. An animal approaching you does not mean it wants to be hugged, chased, touched on the antlers or teased with food.

My practical advice is to pause before taking out the crackers. Count how many deer are nearby, make sure you have room to step away, and notice whether young children are beside you. Keep extra distance from stags in autumn and from mothers with fawns. Never touch a fawn.

How to Buy Shika Senbei

As of August 2026, the official price is ¥200 for a bundle of ten crackers. They are made mainly from wheat flour and rice bran, with no added sugar, and part of the proceeds supports deer protection. Roadside stalls, shops near Tōdai-ji and vendors around the park sell them under signs reading “鹿せんべい.”

Carry coins or small notes and ask for “shika senbei, hitotsu kudasai.” Vendor hours depend on weather, season and foot traffic, so late-afternoon availability is not guaranteed. Vending machines have also been installed at Rokuen and the Kasuga Taisha parking-area rest stop, selling one box of ten. Availability can change, so do not rely on a machine as your only option.

Five Steps for a Calm Feeding Experience

  1. Choose one calm deer. Avoid stepping into the center of a group. Keep at least an arm's length of space.
  2. Remove the paper seal and hide the remaining stack. Store maps, paper and plastic bags securely so they are not mistaken for food.
  3. Feed one cracker at a time, promptly. Hold your hand flat and do not repeatedly pull the cracker away once the deer has seen it.
  4. Do not demand a bow. Some deer bow and others do not. The goal is a respectful interaction, not a performance for the camera.
  5. Show both empty hands when finished. Open your palms toward the deer, then move away calmly. Do not run or wave an empty bag.

Start with one bundle rather than buying several. Ten crackers are enough to learn how quickly the deer approach and how comfortable you feel. You can always buy another bundle later.

The magic is not whether a deer bows; it is that an ancient city has kept a shared path open for wildlife and people.

Rules That Protect Both You and the Deer

  • Feed only official deer crackers—never bread, vegetables, fruit or your own snacks.
  • Take all rubbish with you. Nara Park has no public rubbish bins.
  • Keep away from fawns and protective mothers.
  • Never grab antlers, ride a deer or surprise one from behind.
  • Wash your hands after feeding and before eating.

If several deer surround you, finish or secure the crackers, show your empty palms and move slowly while facing them. With young children, an adult should hold the stack and guide the child's hand rather than giving the child the whole bundle.

Five Nearby Places Worth Your Time

1. Tōdai-ji and the Great Buddha Hall

Walk beneath the vast timber structure of Nandaimon before entering the Great Buddha Hall. In 2026, the hall is open 7:30–17:30 from April to October and 8:00–17:00 from November to March. Individual adult admission is ¥800 and payment is cash only.

2. Nigatsudō

The hillside veranda east of the Great Buddha Hall offers a broad view over Nara. The short climb past stone steps and old walls is atmospheric, free, and easy to add to a Tōdai-ji visit.

3. Mount Wakakusa

The grassy mountain opens from the third Saturday of March to the second Sunday of December, 9:00–17:00. Admission is ¥150 for adults and ¥80 for children aged three and over. Even the first viewpoint is rewarding, but the slope is steeper than it looks.

4. Kasuga Taisha and Tobihino

Vermilion corridors, forest and mossy lanterns reveal the cultural meaning of Nara's sacred deer. The general worship area is free; special inner-area admission is usually 9:00–16:00 and costs ¥700, subject to ceremonies. Nearby Tobihino is a wide lawn where deer can often be observed with more space.

5. Kōfuku-ji, Sarusawa Pond and Naramachi

On the return toward Kintetsu Nara Station, continue through Kōfuku-ji and Sarusawa Pond into Naramachi. Traditional townhouses, cafés, persimmon-leaf sushi and local pickles give the day a human-scale ending after the temples and deer.

Two Suggested Routes

Four-hour classic: Kintetsu Nara Station → Kōfuku-ji → deer feeding → Tōdai-ji and the Great Buddha Hall → Nigatsudō → return to the station.

Seven-hour in-depth day: Kintetsu Nara Station → Kōfuku-ji → deer feeding around Ukigumo Garden → Tōdai-ji → Nigatsudō → Mount Wakakusa → Kasuga Taisha → Tobihino → dinner in Naramachi. Expect roughly 8–10 kilometres of walking.

Final Practical Notes

Nara Park itself is open free of charge, 24 hours a day, all year. It is about an eight-minute walk from Kintetsu Nara Station and around twenty minutes on foot from JR Nara Station. The park covers more than 500 hectares and has no single formal entrance, so comfortable shoes, cash, a zipped bag, water and simple rain gear matter more than squeezing in too many photo stops.

What stayed with me after feeding the deer was not how quickly the crackers disappeared. It was the quiet moment afterward, when I showed my empty hands and the deer returned to the grass beneath temple roofs and forested hills. Nara is not presenting an animal show. It is showing how a city has tried, for more than a thousand years, to leave room for wild animals within everyday life.
